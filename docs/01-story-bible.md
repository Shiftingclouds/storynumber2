# NUIT BLANCHE: Story Bible

*A dark romance in nine nights.* (Spoilers throughout.)

## The pitch

At 2:47 a.m. on a Friday in February, a Montréal night locksmith takes a job from a number he doesn't know. The caller
uses a phrase only his father ever used. The door is under the old British fort on Île Sainte-Hélène, and the lock on it
carries his grandfather's maker's mark. He opens it, because opening things is the one thing he has always been good at.

Behind it is a djinn who has been chained there since 1967. The djinn was the battery for **the Hush**, the spell that
keeps the city's hidden world hidden. Now the Hush is failing. It will be gone in nine nights, on **Nuit blanche**, when
half a million people stay up all night in the streets of Montréal.

Vampires, wolves, witches, a devil who only comes to dances, and the bell-ringing hunters of the Carillon all want
the one mortal on the island who can open their locks. Two men who have hated each other in public for seven years
both want him. In private, they have wanted each other that whole time.

## Tone

- **A dark romance.** Desire is dangerous and it matters. Sex is on the page and it changes things: every intimate
  scene moves a relationship somewhere it can't come back from. Consent is never in doubt, even when the
  dynamics are dark (bites, bargains, power). Everyone is an adult.
- **A thriller.** A clock that never stops (the Hush gauge), a murderer in the city, set pieces every night.
- **Funny.** Montréal is profane, bilingual and absurd. The MC deflects with jokes. Dario swears in three languages.
  A 250-year-old devil runs a nightclub. A djinn from 1967 has never seen a phone.
- **Specific.** Real places, real weather (-24°C with the wind), real folklore. No generic names.

## The theme: the Hush and the closet

The Veillée survives by being unseen, and the Hush is the machinery of that invisibility. The book asks what hiding
costs and who pays for it. The ghost Fleurette died in 1977, the year of the Truxx raid. The two leads are closeted
twice: from the Veillée's laws and from each other. The MC, a gay man who has always been quietly half-hidden, has to
decide whether a whole secret world should step into the light, and whether he will.

Nobody in the book is simply right. The Hush really did stop a war in 1966, and it really is built on stolen children.

## The world

**The Veillée** (*vay-YAY*): what the hidden world calls itself. A *veillée* was the old Québécois evening gathering
where people told stories of loups-garous, flying canoes and the devil at the dance. The Veillée *is* those stories,
still up after midnight. Mortals are **sleepers**.

**The Hush** (*la Berceuse*): a lullaby-spell over the island, cast in 1967 for Expo 67. Sleepers who glimpse the
Veillée forget. It is powered by a stolen djinn's power to *unmake*. It also has a darker use the Compagnie never
admits: **unmaking**. A person can be erased from everyone's memory, or have their own memory erased. Everyone the
Hush has done this to is called *unmade*.

**The Accord of '67**: the treaty that made the Hush. It was signed by the Compagnie, witnessed by a devil, and bought
with a djinn.

### Factions

| Faction | Who | Seat | Wants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Carillon** | Mortal hunters sworn to the Hush. They carry iron and handbells; one rung at a sleeper's temple makes them forget. Their Grand Master is called **the Bourdon**, after the great bell. | The two towers of Notre-Dame Basilica, *La Persévérance* (the Gros Bourdon) and *La Tempérance* (the carillon of ten bells). | To keep the Hush, whatever it costs. |
| **The Beaver Club** | Vampires. A fur-traders' dining club founded in 1785 that never disbanded; its members all "wintered" once. Old anglo money on the mountain. | Strachan House, the Golden Square Mile. | The Hush (so they can feed quietly) and a contract on the key (the MC). |
| **The Sept-Ans** | The loup-garou pack. In Québec folklore, a man who skips his Easter duties for seven years becomes a loup-garou. The pack are the city's excommunicated: queer kids, divorced women, a nun who walked out. Drawing a loup-garou's blood "frees" him and makes him a sleeper again, which is how the Carillon "mercies" wolves. | Église Saint-Jude (patron of lost causes), a deconsecrated church in Saint-Léonard that is now their bar and home, *Chez Jude*. | An end to the Hush's laws, which treat them as vermin. |
| **The Missing Line** | The market. Montréal's métro skips Line 3; it was planned in the 1960s and never built. The Veillée built it. After the last train, a market runs in its ghost stations. Currency is **favors**. | Ghost platforms beneath the city, entered through a door behind the ovens of a 24-hour bagel bakery. | Commerce. Neutrality. The Conductor's word is law. |
| **The Buanderie** | The coven: five old women who run a laundromat in Pointe-Saint-Charles. Spells are cast in the dryers. They fly the **chasse-galerie**, the devil's flying canoe. | Buanderie Pépin. | To be left alone, and for the Compagnie to pay for 1967. |
| **The Compagnie** | The secret society behind the Accord, named for the 17th-century *Compagnie du Saint-Sacrement* whose dévots funded Ville-Marie. Its members are the Bourdon, Honora Strachan and the Conductor. | Wherever the Beaver Club dines. | To rebind the djinn on Nuit blanche. |
| **Le Mardi Gras** | Not a faction: one devil and his club on the Main, where it is always the last hour before Ash Wednesday. | A 1940s cabaret on boulevard Saint-Laurent. | To be invited. |

### Rules that matter to the plot

1. **Locks of the Veillée are made against magic.** No creature of the Veillée can open a lock built to keep
   them out. A mortal hand can, if it knows how. Aurèle Lacroix built the vault's lock in 1967 so that only a Lacroix
   could open *or close* it. That makes the MC the only key on the island, and, if they must, the Compagnie's only way
   to relock it.
2. **The Hush can't take the MC.** Nadim's debt marks him as a creditor of the Veillée, and a creditor can't be
   Hushed. The first bell rung at his temple fails. That's how the Carillon know something is wrong with him.
3. **Djinn law.** A djinn owes his liberator. Nadim's power is to make and to unmake; his wishes can unmake a moment
   (**the undo mechanic**). While he is in debt he cannot refuse a wish, so he cannot consent to anything, and a romance
   with him is only possible after his debt is released. Unspent wishes can be spent on one **great wish** in the
   finale, or given back to set him free.
4. **Loups-garous.** Seven years without Easter. Cutting one and drawing blood lifts the curse. It also makes him a
   sleeper, and the Hush then takes back everything he knew. Dario can never be "saved" without forgetting Enzo.
5. **Demons may only enter where they are invited.** Under the Hush, sleepers can't see Rose to invite him. He has
   not been freely chosen in fifty-nine years.
6. **The chasse-galerie** flies only if no one aboard says a holy word or touches a steeple. Québécois swears are
   holy words (*tabarnak*, *câlice*, *hostie*, *ciboire*). Dario is aboard.
7. **Ghouls** eat a mouthful of the dead and inherit their last memories.
8. **Ghosts** stay for as long as nobody alive remembers their name. Say it where the city can hear, and they can go.

## The cast

All portraits are 64×64 pixel art (see `03-systems.md`). "Hearts" are the relationship meter (regard, from −5
broken hearts to +5 hearts); "flames" are desire, shown only for romanceable characters.

### You: the Fool

**[Name] Lacroix**, 29, he/him. Night locksmith, *Serrurerie Lacroix 24/7*, which his grandfather founded in 1958.
Grew up in Verdun. His father was francophone; his mother, Kath Byrne, was a Newfoundland anglo who died in 2019. He's
fluent in both languages and at home in neither. Out since seventeen. One real relationship (Marc-André, who left for
Toronto) and a lot of three-a.m. nothing since. Drives a 2009 Econoline. Funny as a shield. Good with his hands.
He'll open any door, which is his whole problem.

His father **Serge** "walked out" when he was fourteen. (He didn't. See the Keyman.)

He's written as a real character with a voice. The player picks *which* thing he says. Most dialogue offers four
registers that land differently with each person:

- **Wry**: the joke, the deflection.
- **Earnest**: the plain truth.
- **Bold**: the challenge or the flirt.
- **Guarded**: the lie or the silence.

Lazare distrusts wry and melts at earnest. Dario lights up at wry and bold. Rose respects refusals. Nadim likes wry
and hates being pitied. Fleurette lives for wry.

### The two leads (the midpoint split)

**Lazare Desautels**, 31. Hunter of the Carillon. *Born Lorenzo "Enzo" Ferrante.*
Tall and severe, with dark curls kept too short and a scar on his chin he doesn't remember getting. He wears a
long wool coat, carries iron in his sleeves, and a brass handbell on a leather cord against his chest. He was raised in
the bell tower from the age of ten, and believes a loup-garou killed his parents. He's precise and dry, devout without
certainty, and kind in ways he hides. He has been sleeping with his sworn enemy for seven years and calls it a
tactical relationship.
*The truth:* the Carillon took him from his parents' house on rue Jarry in Saint-Léonard in 2005, and the Hush unmade
him from his family's memory. His parents, Rosa and Vito, are alive and don't know he existed. His best friend on that
street was Dario Santangelo.
*Wants:* to be good. *Fears:* that he isn't. *Romance:* earnest and slow, then devastating. Consent is spoken aloud;
he asks, every time.

**Dario Santangelo**, 31. Alpha of the Sept-Ans.
Broad, bearded and loud, with a broken nose, a gold chain and a beer-league goalie's knees. He drives a tow truck. He
swears in French, English and Italian, sometimes in one sentence. At twelve, a priest told him boys like him were
damned, so he stopped going to Mass. Seven years later, at nineteen, he turned, and *remembered*: the change broke the
Hush for him and gave him back a best friend nobody else remembered existed. He went looking, and found a Carillon
hunter named Lazare, who tried to kill him on a roof in Rosemont. The first time Dario called him *Enzo*, Lazare put a
silver knife through his shoulder. Dario kept the name as a pet name. Lazare thinks it's a taunt.
*Wants:* Enzo back. *Fears:* that he's already had all he'll get. *Romance:* loud, hungry and funny, with a
tenderness that ambushes you.

Their public rivalry is real: rooftop fights and ten years of insults. Their private affair is also real. Neither has
ever said the word love. The MC walks into the middle of it.

### The others you can fall for

**Rose, le Beau Danseur.** A demon. In 1740 he came to a Mardi Gras dance in a fine black coat and gloves and danced
with a girl called Rose Latulipe until midnight. The legend says the priest drove him off. The truth is that she said
no, and he has been in love with that refusal for 286 years. He took her name. He's beautiful, courteous and amused,
never lies, and bargains for everything. Gloves always on. His club, Le Mardi Gras, is on the Main. He wants to be
*invited*. His romance is temptation that turns out to be about consent: he will only take what you give him.

**Nadim.** A djinn of smokeless fire, from the hills above Beirut, before Beirut had that name. He was sold at the
Missing Line in 1958 and bound under the fort in 1967. He's formal, sardonic and very old, and his cultural references
stop at the summer of Expo ("Is the monorail still running?"). He's weak and furious, and it's easy to underestimate
how much he's grieving. He owes the MC. He can't say no to a wish, so he says as little as possible. Romance only
after his debt is released. It is the slowest burn in the book.

**Ruari Strachan** (a fling, not a route). A vampire, Honora's fledgling, turned in 1998 at a rave in a Verdun
warehouse. Scottish vowels, kandi bracelets, gorgeous and bored. A feeding scene with him is one of the steamiest in
the book. He is also the murderer.

### Allies, enemies, everyone else

- **Madame Fleurette** (Réal Gauthier, 1941–1977): a ghost. A drag queen who ruled a Stanley Street cabaret in the
  seventies. Her heart gave out the night of the Truxx raid, in October 1977, on the sidewalk, watching the vans. She
  haunts the jukebox at Chez Normande, a Village dive. She is the first to notice the MC can see her. She's a
  gossip, a mentor and bitchy with love. She's the **"Ask Fleurette"** feature: the dead hear everything said in bars.
  She wants her name said where the city can hear it.
- **Aimé Bélanger**, 29. A ghoul. The undertaker's son from Verdun (*Salon funéraire Bélanger & Fils*), who sat
  behind the MC in Secondaire 3 and has had a crush on him since. He eats a mouthful of the dead and inherits their
  last memories. He sells memories at the Missing Line and hates himself a little for it. He loves true-crime podcasts,
  is terrible at lying, and is loyal to the bone.
- **Gisèle Pépin**, 71. Witch and head of the Buanderie coven. She chain-smokes du Maurier, curses like a sailor and
  plays bingo on Tuesdays. She pilots the chasse-galerie. In 1966 she was the lover of Aurèle Lacroix, the MC's
  grandfather. When he built the lock for the Compagnie, she never forgave him. She can weave a new Hush, but only
  with consent.
- **Honora Strachan**: President of the Beaver Club, turned in 1812, the widow of a North West Company partner. She's
  small and very polite, her manners are a scalpel, and she's very tired of the twentieth century. A member of the
  Compagnie. She ordered the "tidying" that became the murders, and she can be turned.
- **Everett Clarke**, *the Conductor*. A warlock: someone who cheated a demon out of the price of his power. A
  Barbadian-born Montréal sleeping-car porter, in 1911 he won his soul back from a demon in a card game somewhere past
  Kingston. He runs the Missing Line and is a member of the Compagnie. In 1958, he sold Nadim. He has regretted it for
  sixty-eight years, which isn't the same as making it right.
- **The Keyman**: a nameless, gentle key-cutter at a stall on the Missing Line. He hums the same six notes over and
  over. He is **Serge Lacroix**, unmade in 2011 when he tried to free Nadim. As the Hush weakened, fragments came back:
  a son, a combination, a phrase. **He made the call.**
- **Lucille "Mémé" Lacroix**, 88. The MC's grandmother, Aurèle's widow, in a care home in Verdun with dementia. At three
  in the morning she's lucid, and she knows things.
- **Brother Clément Ouimet, the Bourdon.** Grand Master of the Carillon, 74, who raised Lazare. Gentle, tired and
  sincere. He believes the Hush saved ten thousand lives, and he may be right. He chose the children to take. He
  signed the Accord at twenty-two. He's the antagonist, and he isn't a hypocrite.
- **Sister Agathe Marchand**, 33. Lazare's hunting partner in the Carillon. She's loyal, funny in a flat way, the
  closest thing he has to a sister, and a true believer. A bell went missing from her armory.
- **Manon Lefebvre**, 46. Dario's second. She was a nun until she walked out of the convent in 2009; the pack found
  her seven years later. She's the calmest person in any room, and bites.
- **Jean-Baptiste**: the angel in the Gros Bourdon, the eleven-ton bell of Notre-Dame, cast in 1848. It has objected
  to the Hush since 1967. The Carillon's changes are rung to drown it out. An angel's help is real, and so is its
  idea of justice, which does not do mercy the way humans want.

## The mystery: the Quiet Killings

As the Hush fails, the unmade begin to remember, and somebody is killing them before they can talk. Each victim has a
bruise at the temple shaped like the lip of a bell (a Carillon Hush-bell) and grey wolf hair in their fist.

- **Truth:** Ruari Strachan, on Honora's order to "tidy" witnesses, with a Hush-bell stolen from the Carillon armory and
  a wolf pelt from the Beaver Club's trophy room. The bell hides a bite. The wolf hair frames the pack. The Bourdon
  supplied the list of names. The aim is to start a war between the Carillon and the pack, and war justifies the
  rebinding.
- **Red herrings:** the pack (wolf hair), Lazare (bell bruise, and he was absent on the second night), Rose (he was
  at the first scene).
- The victims include people the MC has met. The Keyman is on the list.

See `02-storyboard.md` for clues and deductions.

## Tropes, and what we do with them

| Trope | Done straight, done well | Twisted |
|---|---|---|
| The Chosen One | The MC is special | ...because he's the most ordinary person there. The locks were built against magic. |
| Rivals to lovers | Lazare × Dario, seven years of it | The MC walks in on the middle of their story, not the start |
| Love triangle | Two men want the MC | They also want each other. The best ending is a throuple, and it is hard to earn. |
| Hunter vs. monster | The Carillon hunts wolves | The hunter was stolen by his own order; the monster is the only one who remembered him |
| The seductive devil | Rose tempts | What he wants most is to be refused properly, or chosen freely |
| The genie's three wishes | Nadim owes wishes | Each one is a moment you take back. The last can remake the world, or free him. |
| The wise mentor | Fleurette teaches the MC the ropes | She's a drag queen who died in 1977 and she wants something for herself |
| The secret society | The Compagnie | Its leader is sincere, and not entirely wrong |
| The masquerade | The Hush hides the Veillée | The book asks whether it should. The finale is a real choice with real costs either way. |
| The absent father | Serge walked out | He was unmade, and he's been cutting keys ten metres below his son's feet |
| Amnesia | Lazare doesn't remember | ...and the reveal is the midpoint. It lands on both men, and on the MC. |

## The midpoint twist: the Thaw (end of Night Four)

At midnight at Rose's party, the failing Hush lets go for one hour. For sixty minutes, everything it unmade comes
back. Sleepers all over the island see the Veillée, and every stolen memory returns.

1. **Lazare remembers he is Enzo Ferrante.** His mother's kitchen on rue Jarry, a boy with a broken nose on the next
   balcony. His parents are alive. The Carillon took him, and the man he has been sleeping with for seven years knew all
   along.
2. **The MC remembers** being twelve, at the fort, with his father singing him six notes: the combination. His own father
   Hushed him to protect him.
3. **The game itself thaws.** From Night One the journal has shown *hushed* lines as blacked-out text. At the Thaw they
   melt. Some stay readable and some freeze again when the hour ends.

At one in the morning, when the Hush closes over the city again, Lazare walks out to face the man who raised him, and
the Carillon declares war on the Sept-Ans for the Thaw. The MC has to choose which man to follow:

- **Night Five and Six (A), the Bells:** with Lazare, into the Carillon, the Register of stolen children, and the
  angel in the bell.
- **Night Five and Six (B), the Wolves:** with Dario, into the besieged pack, the market war, and the chasse-galerie
  flight to take Enzo back.

The man you don't follow acts on his own, offscreen, according to how much he trusted you, and you find out
how.

## Endings, in brief

Twenty-two endings (see `02-storyboard.md`), built from four things:

- **What happens to the Hush:** rebound, replaced by the MC, fallen, remade by consent, held by the devil, or undone by
  a great wish.
- **Whom the MC loves:** Lazare, Dario, both, Rose, Nadim, or no one.
- **What the MC becomes:** still mortal, a wolf, a vampire, the devil's consort, the lock itself, dead, or Hushed.
- **Who lives.**

Every ending is followed by **Morning**, a flag-driven epilogue with a portrait for each person you met. It's all
written in advance and works offline.
